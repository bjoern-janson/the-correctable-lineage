# Research Claim Boundary

## Status

This is the one-page claim boundary for the current research program.

It supersedes broader interpretations of the corpus when they conflict with this document.

---

## Primary research claim

The project proposes the following testable hypothesis:

> Explicit representation of identified content, typed authority, validity scope, evidence dependencies, and reopening conditions may reduce invalid authority transfer during sequential evidence updates.

In compact form:

\[
\boxed{
\text{identified content}
\rightarrow
\text{typed and scoped authority update}
}
\]

The governing constraint is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Authority may increase only over claims whose content is invariant under the remaining observational ambiguity.}
}
\]

This is a governance hypothesis anchored in established identifiability theory.

It is not yet an established comparative result.

---

## Established substrate

The project treats the following as established or substantially established outside the corpus:

- statistical and causal identifiability;
- impossibility under observational equivalence;
- point and partial identification;
- identified sets and equivalence classes;
- finite-sample estimation and uncertainty;
- Bayesian and robust decision theory;
- causal transportability and intervention;
- belief revision;
- assurance cases, defeaters, and monitoring arguments;
- provenance and data-lineage methods.

The factorization condition:

\[
O(f_a)=O(f_b)
\Longrightarrow
L(f_a)=L(f_b)
\]

or:

\[
L=\widehat L\circ O
\]

is treated as a standard target/query identifiability criterion expressed in corpus notation, not as a new theorem.

---

## Proposed objects

The project studies whether correction improves when commitments explicitly record:

\[
C=(H,W,\Sigma,\Pi,\mathcal R,\Gamma),
\]

where:

- \(H\): claim or hypothesis;
- \(W\): typed epistemic or operational authority;
- \(\Sigma\): validity and action scope;
- \(\Pi\): evidence provenance and shared dependencies;
- \(\mathcal R\): reopening conditions;
- \(\Gamma\): required response when reopening occurs.

Decision authority is kept separate from epistemic authority and depends on a declared loss or consequence model \(\Lambda\).

---

## What is currently supported

The synthetic benchmarks v0.1–v0.5 show only that the supplied representation can encode and avoid selected evaluator-authored governance errors under frozen toy conditions.

They support representational sufficiency within those conditions.

They do not establish human usability, transfer, necessity, comparative superiority, or autonomous discovery.

Pilot 0 is designed to test whether independent users can instantiate and revise the representation without inventor arbitration.

No Pilot 0 participant data exist yet.

---

## What is not claimed

The project does not currently claim:

- a new theory of identifiability;
- a replacement for Bayesian, causal, robust, belief-revision, provenance, or assurance methods;
- a universal theory of rationality, intelligence, governance, or corrigibility;
- a universal authority metric, scope map, governance loss, or reopening rule;
- that claim contracts are necessary rather than one possible synthesis;
- that explicit bookkeeping outperforms mature alternatives;
- autonomous discovery of hypotheses, scopes, dependencies, losses, interventions, or correction paths;
- AGI alignment, containment, moral conversion, or strategic-deception detection;
- that any present AI system has a particular moral character or stable hidden objective;
- that distributed oversight alone guarantees truth or safety.

---

## Required external evidence

The central comparative question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does explicit authority bookkeeping improve correction behavior relative to the strongest fair alternatives after complexity cost?}
}
\]

Evidence must come from:

- independently authored cases;
- method-faithful comparators;
- users without framework-author mediation;
- plural and revisable adjudication;
- measured translation and complexity costs;
- precommitted defeat, absorption, and narrowing outcomes.

---

## Defeat and absorption conditions

The project loses authority as a distinct operational contribution if:

1. mature methods or minimal method-native augmentations reproduce the same correction behavior at equal or lower cost;
2. outsiders cannot instantiate the representation reliably;
3. apparent advantages disappear on independently authored cases;
4. gains depend on framework vocabulary or author arbitration;
5. generalized abstention explains the result;
6. evaluator-selected losses determine the winner without robustness;
7. the representation adds terminology without new discrimination, coordination, or correction value.

A valid outcome may be:

- a useful synthesis layer;
- a pedagogical decomposition;
- a domain-specific assurance tool;
- absorption into an established method;
- no demonstrated operational advantage.

---

## Current scientific frontier

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can the representation survive removal of its creator as an implicit interpretation layer?}
}
\]

Until that is tested, the work remains a candidate research instrument rather than an externally validated one.
