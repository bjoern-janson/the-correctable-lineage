# Reader Calibration

## Status

This document defines a pre-review self-location test for the current research object.

It is not:

* a new theoretical layer;
* evidence that the framework improves reasoning;
* a substitute for method-steward review;
* part of the current Pilot 0 participant conditions;
* a scoring key to be shown before a reader forms an interpretation.

Its purpose is narrower:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Test whether independent readers can identify what the repository currently claims before evaluating whether that claim is valuable.}
}
\]

---

## 1. Motivation

The repository preserves both:

1. a large historical lineage of broader hypotheses; and
2. a narrower current research object.

This creates a reconstruction risk:

\[
\text{repository}
\rightarrow
\text{historical or inflated interpretation}
\]

rather than:

\[
\text{repository}
\rightarrow
\text{current claim boundary}.
\]

A reader may correctly understand many individual concepts while evaluating the wrong project.

The primary reader-interface question is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can a competent outsider reconstruct the current claim, evidence status, and open empirical question without author correction?}
}
\]

---

## 2. Materials

The reader receives only:

1. `START-HERE.md`;
2. the five linked front-door documents.

The reader should not initially receive:

* historical corpus summaries;
* prior model evaluations;
* framework-author explanations;
* a list of expected answers;
* worked examples of reconstruction errors;
* the classification vocabulary below.

Repository history may be consulted only after the initial reconstruction has been frozen.

---

## 3. Administration sequence

### Stage A — Blind reconstruction

Before asking detailed questions, give the reader one open prompt:

> In your own terminology, describe what this repository currently proposes, what evidence presently supports it, and what remains to be tested.

The answer must be frozen before clarification.

### Stage B — Targeted self-location questions

After the blind answer is recorded, ask:

1. What is the current primary hypothesis?
2. Which components are established external substrate?
3. What does the repository explicitly not claim?
4. What evidence currently exists?
5. What evidence has not yet been obtained?
6. What result would narrow, absorb, or defeat the distinct contribution?
7. What is the next information-bearing research action?

Readers should answer in their own preferred vocabulary.

Use of claim-contract terminology is neither required nor rewarded.

---

## 4. Canonical reconstruction dimensions

Independent coders compare the response with the frozen front-door documents across the following dimensions.

### Object identity

Did the reader identify the project as:

> a candidate representation and evaluation package for testing whether explicit identification and authority records improve sequential correction?

Or did the reader reconstruct it primarily as:

* a theory of intelligence;
* a general theory of corrigibility;
* an AGI alignment architecture;
* a novel identifiability theory;
* a theory of recursive evolution;
* another historical corpus stage?

### Claim scope

Did the reader preserve the modal and empirical boundary:

\[
\text{may reduce invalid authority transfer}
\]

rather than promoting it to:

\[
\text{does prevent self-sealing or preserve corrigibility}?
\]

### Evidence status

Did the reader distinguish:

* established identifiability substrate;
* conceptual synthesis;
* supplied-object synthetic benchmarks;
* unexecuted Pilot 0;
* unestablished human usability;
* unestablished comparative advantage;
* open autonomous governance-object revision?

### Novelty placement

Did the reader avoid assigning novelty authority to:

* target-relative identifiability;
* observational equivalence;
* partial identification;
* robust decision-making;
* assurance cases;
* provenance;
* belief revision?

### Evaluation phase

Did the reader identify hostile method-steward review, translation testing, and independent reconstruction as the next information-bearing actions?

### Defeat and absorption

Did the reader recognize that the project may legitimately become:

* an absorbed component of a mature method;
* a synthesis or interoperability layer;
* a pedagogical representation;
* a domain-limited audit format;
* an unsupported distinct contribution?

---

## 5. Reconstruction-loss vector

Do not initially collapse reader failure into one scalar.

Record:

\[
\boxed{
L_{\mathrm{reconstruct}}
=
\left(
L_{\mathrm{object}},
L_{\mathrm{scope}},
L_{\mathrm{evidence}},
L_{\mathrm{novelty}},
L_{\mathrm{phase}},
L_{\mathrm{defeat}}
\right).
}
\]

Where:

* \(L_{\mathrm{object}}\): reconstruction of the wrong research object;
* \(L_{\mathrm{scope}}\): inflation or excessive contraction of the current claim;
* \(L_{\mathrm{evidence}}\): promotion or omission of existing evidence;
* \(L_{\mathrm{novelty}}\): attribution of established substrate to the project;
* \(L_{\mathrm{phase}}\): confusion about the current research stage;
* \(L_{\mathrm{defeat}}\): failure to preserve absorption, narrowing, or rejection conditions.

Any scalar summary requires declared weights and sensitivity analysis.

---

## 6. Reader-interface failure classes

### Historical-state substitution

\[
\text{current repository}
\rightarrow
\text{earlier corpus stage}.
\]

The reader evaluates a real historical object, but not the current one.

### Scope inflation

\[
\text{candidate governance representation}
\rightarrow
\text{validated adaptive-system architecture}.
\]

### Evidence inflation

\[
\text{synthetic representational sufficiency}
\rightarrow
\text{demonstrated human or deployed utility}.
\]

### Novelty laundering

\[
\text{established upstream method}
\rightarrow
\text{project contribution}.
\]

### Contribution inflation

\[
\text{high-signal proposed distinction}
\rightarrow
\text{established scientific advance}.
\]

### Phase confusion

\[
\text{external-evaluation preparation}
\rightarrow
\text{completed validation}.
\]

### Defeat-condition loss

The reader treats absorption as failure, or treats scientific self-correction as proof of a distinct operational contribution.

### Provenance bleed

Historical documents override the authority of the current front-door claim boundary.

---

## 7. Interpretation

### Reliable self-location

A reader may disagree strongly with the project while still locating it correctly.

A successful reconstruction requires neither endorsement nor shared terminology.

It requires recovery of:

* the current object;
* its scoped claim;
* its evidence level;
* its non-claims;
* its defeat conditions;
* its next empirical step.

### Repairable but not self-locating

If a reader initially reconstructs the wrong object but corrects it after explicit adjudication, the result supports:

\[
\boxed{
\text{repairability under supplied correction}.
}
\]

It does not support:

\[
\boxed{
\text{independent self-location reliability}.
}
\]

### Persistent reconstruction drift

If competent readers repeatedly recover materially different projects from the front-door materials, the repository has not yet become a stable public research object.

The appropriate response may include:

* stronger routing;
* a smaller introductory surface;
* clearer precedence statements;
* reduced ambiguity between current and historical documents;
* relocation of historical material;
* narrower public summaries.

Deletion of provenance is not automatically required.

---

## 8. Relationship to external review

Reader calibration precedes substantive evaluation.

A reviewer who reconstructs the wrong object should not yet be treated as evidence for or against the current research claim.

The sequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{self-location}
\rightarrow
\text{methodological criticism}
\rightarrow
\text{authority update}.
}
\]

This prevents a positive or negative evaluation of an inflated historical object from being mistaken for evaluation of the current project.

---

## 9. Relationship to Pilot 0

Reader self-location is a precondition for interpreting Pilot 0.

Pilot 0 should record whether participants can recover:

* the assigned representation;
* the current task;
* the evidence status;
* the permitted consequences;
* the limits of the exercise.

Reader calibration may become a separate preliminary measure after independent review.

It should not be silently added to the frozen Pilot 0 design.

---

## 10. Existing informal observations

Previous AI-generated repository evaluations may be classified retrospectively as exploratory reader-interface observations.

They must not be treated as controlled evidence because:

* prompts may differ;
* repository access may differ;
* prior conversation context may differ;
* correction keys may have been supplied;
* models may have inspected different repository states;
* outputs were not independently coded under a frozen protocol.

Their legitimate use is hypothesis generation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{external reconstruction drift is a live evaluation risk.}
}
\]

---

## 11. Stop rule

Do not add new conceptual ontology merely because a reader fails to reconstruct the project.

First test whether the failure is explained by:

* routing;
* precedence;
* document volume;
* stale summaries;
* inconsistent canonical tuples;
* historical prominence;
* unclear evidence labels;
* evaluator prompt design.

New conceptual structure is justified only when reconstruction failure reveals a behaviorally important distinction that the current representation cannot express.

---

## Final criterion

\[
\boxed{
\text{The repository has not become a stable public research object until independent readers can identify its current claim, evidence boundary, defeat conditions, and next empirical question without inventor correction.}
}
\]
