# Architecture of the Correctable Lineage

## Overview

The corpus converges on an architecture with two coupled systems:

\[
\boxed{
\text{adaptive transformation}
+
\text{epistemic governance}
}
\]

The first changes the system.

The second determines when those changes are warranted, how deeply they may propagate, and what evidence survives the change.

---

## I. Adaptive transformation loop

\[
\text{Reality}
\rightarrow
\text{Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Representation}
\rightarrow
\text{Search}
\rightarrow
\text{Action}
\rightarrow
\text{Consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{Revision}
\rightarrow
\text{Inheritance}
\]

### 1. Reality

The external process that produces consequences not fully authored by the current adaptive regime.

### 2. Observation

The interface through which target-relevant distinctions become available.

Observation does not passively reveal reality. It induces an equivalence relation:

\[
f_a \sim_O f_b
\iff
O(f_a)=O(f_b).
\]

Anything collapsed by this relation is unavailable to downstream reasoning through that interface.

### 3. Representation

The coordinate system in which observations become patterns, invariants, predictions, and possible actions.

Representations determine:

- what is visible;
- what is compressible;
- what operations are cheap;
- which hypotheses are expressible;
- which solutions are reachable.

### 4. Search

The transformation of representation into candidate actions, explanations, or mechanisms.

Search efficiency and reachable possibility are relational properties of the task, representation, architecture, and resource budget.

### 5. Action

The system changes the environment or its own internal organization.

Action changes not only outcomes but future evidence availability.

### 6. Consequence

Reality returns differences between expected and realized outcomes.

A consequence becomes useful only when it can be validated, attributed, and routed to an addressable source of future behavior.

### 7. Revision

The responsible mechanism, representation, authority allocation, or update rule changes.

The desired principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{change the lowest sufficient causal layer}
}
\]

### 8. Inheritance

A successful correction becomes part of the substrate from which later adaptation begins.

Inheritance must preserve not only the selected mechanism, but its evidence, validity domain, unresolved uncertainty, and revision conditions.

---

## II. Epistemic governance loop

The governance loop prevents adaptive transformation from certifying itself.

\[
\text{target declaration}
\rightarrow
\text{interface declaration}
\rightarrow
\text{identifiability}
\rightarrow
\text{estimation}
\rightarrow
\text{prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{intervention}
\rightarrow
\text{provenance}
\]

### 1. Target declaration

What property is being studied?

A system cannot infer target legitimacy from measurement success alone.

### 2. Interface declaration

What observations are available, at what cost, timing, precision, and repetition budget?

### 3. Factorization

For class \(F\), interface \(O\), and target \(L\), ask whether:

\[
L=\widehat L\circ O.
\]

If not, the target is not identifiable through the declared interface.

### 4. Estimation

When factorization exists, ask whether finite data can estimate \(\widehat L\) with declared uncertainty.

### 5. Predictive validity

Ask whether the estimated target adds held-out predictive value beyond preregistered baselines.

### 6. Causal intervention

Ask whether a candidate mechanism causally changes the validated target.

### 7. Provenance

Record:

- what was tested;
- under which class and interface;
- what evidence exists;
- what failed;
- what remains unresolved;
- which downstream claims remain unauthorized.

---

## III. Correction pipeline

The direct loop:

\[
\text{Error}\rightarrow\text{Update}
\]

is insufficient for deep adaptive systems.

The corpus instead converges on:

\[
\text{Error}
\rightarrow
\text{Validation}
\rightarrow
\text{Competing Attributions}
\rightarrow
\text{Permission}
\rightarrow
\text{Plasticity Allocation}
\rightarrow
\text{Revision}
\rightarrow
\text{Retest}
\rightarrow
\text{Conditional Inheritance}.
\]

### Epistemic holding state

When attribution confidence is insufficient:

\[
FA_c<\tau_c
\Rightarrow
\lambda_A\rightarrow0.
\]

The contradiction is preserved, but destructive revision is delayed until discriminating evidence becomes available.

A holding state is not denial or inactivity. It is a controlled refusal to convert unresolved evidence into irreversible change.

---

## IV. Error addressability

Let commitments be:

\[
C_t=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)
\]

and residuals:

\[
e_t=(e_1,\ldots,e_m).
\]

The correction topology can be represented conceptually by:

\[
J_{ij}
=
\frac{\partial c_{i,t+1}}
{\partial e_{j,t}}.
\]

The desired correction map is:

- nonzero where evidence implicates a responsible dependency;
- sparse enough to preserve unrelated structure;
- deep enough to reach the true failure source;
- calibrated to evidence strength;
- itself open to revision.

---

## V. Causal separability

Local correction requires a decomposition:

\[
S_t=(M_{1,t},\ldots,M_{n,t},\mathcal I_t)
\]

with three properties.

### Observational separability

Different failures produce distinguishable evidence.

### Interventional separability

One mechanism can be modified without uncontrolled global rewriting.

### Inheritance separability

The descendants of a mechanism remain traceable enough for later revalidation or rollback.

When these conditions fail, attribution becomes decomposition-relative and local correction may be impossible.

---

## VI. Refactoring loop

The causal decomposition itself may become inadequate.

Then the system must move from:

\[
M_i\rightarrow M_i'
\]

within a fixed decomposition to:

\[
\mathcal D_t
\rightarrow
\mathcal D_{t+1}
\]

where \(\mathcal D_t\) is the current factorization of mechanisms and responsibilities.

This is the difference between:

- correcting a component;
- changing what counts as a component.

Refactoring requires migration maps that preserve evidence, authority, validity boundaries, and unresolved contradictions across changing ontologies.

---

## VII. Lineage identity

The correctable lineage does not require every mechanism, representation, target, or institution to remain fixed.

Its candidate identity lies in preserving a relation:

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{corr}}:
\text{validated consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{proportional improvement of future adaptation}.
\]

A mechanism may be replaced while lineage identity survives.

A mechanism may persist while lineage identity is lost if it becomes unreachable by valid correction.

---

## VIII. The complete architecture

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\textbf{Reality}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Interface / Observation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Representation / Compression}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Search / Action / Causal Reach}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Consequence}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Validation / Attribution}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Authority / Permission / Revision}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{Inheritance / Future Adaptive Capacity}\\
\circlearrowleft
\end{array}
}
\]

Around this loop sits the governance structure:

\[
\boxed{
(F,O,L)
+
\text{no-bypass gates}
+
\text{provenance}
+
\text{counterfactuals}
+
\text{claim boundaries}
}
\]

The research object is the lawful coupling of these two systems.
