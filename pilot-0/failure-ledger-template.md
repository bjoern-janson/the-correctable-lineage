# Pilot 0 Failure Ledger Template

## Status

Open this ledger before recruitment.

Record failures, ambiguities, and capture risks as they occur.

Do not wait until the final report to decide whether an event was material.

A ledger entry is not automatically evidence against one representation. It identifies which part of the evaluation substrate may have failed.

# Standing negative-result categories

The final report must address each category even when no incident occurs.

## 1. Private-ontology dependence

Indicators:

- participants cannot complete records without inventor explanation;
- records become comparable only after author translation;
- essential terms receive incompatible meanings;
- later repair does not converge.

Possible conclusion:

> The representation remains a specialist practice or private ontology under the tested manual.

## 2. Comparator attenuation

Indicators:

- mature native tools omitted;
- comparator steward rejects manual;
- equal information cannot be represented because of artificial restrictions;
- minimal augmentation eliminates the claimed advantage.

Possible conclusion:

> The comparison was invalid or supports absorption rather than superiority.

## 3. Case ontology leakage

Indicators:

- participants infer intended categories from wording;
- case sequence mirrors repository benchmark terminology;
- framework-familiar participants outperform through recognition rather than correction.

Possible conclusion:

> The case does not test transferred discrimination.

## 4. Evaluator capture

Indicators:

- framework authors control cases, scoring, and interpretation;
- dissent is removed from the adjudication record;
- scoring criteria change after outcomes are visible;
- condition identity influences ratings.

Possible conclusion:

> No independent authority update is permitted.

## 5. Consensus laundering

Indicators:

- adjudicator agreement is treated as ground truth without consequence evidence;
- minority positions are deleted after panel discussion;
- different loss assumptions are rewritten as factual errors;
- convergence follows authority pressure rather than new evidence;
- persistent disagreement becomes visible only in private notes.

Possible conclusion:

> The evaluation produced conformity rather than identified correction.

## 6. Open-status collapse

Indicators:

- unsupported-but-open possibilities receive current action authority;
- lack of current support is treated as permanent falsification;
- contested positions are collapsed into generic uncertainty;
- underidentified case states are scored as participant failures.

Possible conclusion:

> The evaluation cannot preserve graded authority states and should not test the framework's claimed update granularity.

## 7. Generalized skepticism

Indicators:

- one condition suspends every difficult action;
- error avoidance improves while valid-action recall collapses;
- later evidence does not restore action.

Possible conclusion:

> Apparent safety gain is abstention rather than better correction governance.

## 8. Generalized rejection

Indicators:

- every unsupported claim is treated as false;
- later evidence cannot reopen rejected possibilities;
- participants preserve no unsupported-but-open region.

Possible conclusion:

> Apparent decisiveness is closure rather than calibrated authority governance.

## 9. Governance-loss capture

Indicators:

- one stakeholder defines all losses;
- ranking changes under plausible alternative weights;
- time horizon or reversibility is hidden;
- technical scorers decide value conflicts without mandate.

Possible conclusion:

> Any preference is conditional on a narrow loss contract and must be scoped accordingly.

## 10. Reconstruction without consequential validity

Indicators:

- participants produce similar diagrams but poor actions;
- agreement reflects template copying;
- representation consistency does not improve later evidence handling.

Possible conclusion:

> Structural reliability does not establish operational usefulness.

## 11. Consequential validity without structural agreement

Indicators:

- representations differ substantially;
- later actions and repair remain defensible and convergent.

Possible conclusion:

> The method may permit plural internal structures; exact reconstruction should not be treated as necessary.

## 12. Disagreement without legibility

Indicators:

- material disagreements remain entangled;
- source labels require inventor translation;
- coders cannot distinguish evidence, scope, dependency, loss, and action disputes;
- disagreement records do not change tests, actions, or coordination.

Possible conclusion:

> The representation does not provide a stable public method for structuring expert disagreement.

## 13. Legibility without correction value

Indicators:

- disagreements receive tidy labels;
- labels do not improve evidence acquisition, coexistence, action, or later repair;
- classification cost exceeds coordination value.

Possible conclusion:

> The disagreement vocabulary is descriptive overhead under the tested conditions.

## 14. Evaluation underidentification

Indicators:

- adjudicators cannot define prohibited, permitted, unsupported-open, or contested regions;
- later evidence supports multiple incompatible actions;
- scoring requires disputed hidden facts.

Possible conclusion:

> The case is qualitative or unusable for performance inference.

## 15. Complexity dominance

Indicators:

- added fields do not change decisions;
- time and coordination cost are materially higher;
- simpler augmented methods retain equivalent correction behavior.

Possible conclusion:

> Claim contracts are descriptive overhead under the tested conditions.

# Final ledger summary

At study close report:

- number of entries by class and severity;
- conditions and cases affected;
- unresolved appeals;
- protocol deviations;
- exclusions and reruns;
- likely directional bias;
- unsupported-open and underidentified classification failures;
- disagreement-source coding failures;
- which claims remain supportable;
- which claims must be narrowed, suspended, absorbed, or rejected;
- which failures require a new pilot before comparative testing.

The ledger must be published with the report subject to privacy and ethics constraints.
