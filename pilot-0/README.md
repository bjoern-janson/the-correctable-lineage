# Pilot 0 — Reconstruction and Evaluation-Charter Stress Test

## Status

Pilot 0 is a feasibility package for testing whether outsiders can instantiate and use the proposed governance representations.

It is not:

- benchmark v0.6;
- a comparative-superiority study;
- a preregistered experiment;
- a completed human-participant study;
- evidence that claim contracts improve reasoning;
- authorization to recruit participants.

No participant data have been collected.

Human execution requires independent review, applicable ethics and data-governance approval, consent procedures, and a frozen preregistration.

---

## Primary question

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can independent users instantiate and revise the representations without framework-author arbitration?}
}
\]

Pilot 0 tests the transition:

\[
\text{private ontology}
\rightarrow
\text{public instrument}.
\]

It does not test general comparative superiority.

## Proposed minimal execution

### Participants

- 5–10 researchers or practitioners;
- no requirement that they know the corpus;
- record domain and methods expertise;
- exclude framework authors from participant scoring and primary adjudication.

### Conditions

At minimum:

1. claim contract;
2. mature Bayesian workflow;
3. mature assurance argument.

Participants receive one representation manual only.

The pilot is too small for a stable superiority claim. Condition comparisons are descriptive feasibility signals.

### Cases

- one scientific-replication case;
- one engineering-assurance case;
- one ML-deployment case.

Evidence is released sequentially.

### Primary feasibility endpoints

1. representation completion rate;
2. malformed-field rate;
3. clarification requests;
4. completion time;
5. reconstruction entropy;
6. repair convergence after later evidence;
7. consequential action quality;
8. adjudicator agreement and contested-region size;
9. participant-reported translation burden;
10. evidence of framework vocabulary leakage.

---

## Go/no-go interpretation

### Go to a larger comparative study

Only when:

- outsiders can complete the representations without author intervention;
- comparator stewards accept the manuals as method-faithful;
- neutral cases do not reveal the intended ontology;
- plural adjudication can distinguish prohibited, permitted, and contested actions;
- later evidence produces measurable revision opportunities;
- complexity and translation costs can be recorded reliably;
- defeat conditions remain operational.

### Revise Pilot 0

When:

- manuals are underspecified;
- reconstruction entropy is high but repair convergence remains unclear;
- cases are too obvious or too ambiguous;
- adjudication depends on claim-contract vocabulary;
- one comparator is materially weakened;
- participants need extensive inventor mediation.

### Stop or narrow the operational claim

When:

- outsiders cannot instantiate the representation reliably;
- author arbitration is required to convert divergent records into apparent agreement;
- method-native augmentations reproduce every useful distinction more cheaply;
- no consequence-based scoring can be agreed without embedding the framework ontology.

---

## Governance loss in Pilot 0

Pilot 0 does not assume one universal loss.

For each case, record:

\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{case}}
=
\Lambda(
\text{stakeholders},
\text{domain},
\text{time horizon},
\text{consequence model}
).
\]

The pilot asks whether each representation makes those assumptions visible and revisable.

It does not declare one stakeholder weighting correct.

---

## Final boundary

A successful Pilot 0 could support only:

> Under the tested manuals, cases, participants, and adjudication process, outsiders were able to instantiate and revise the representations with measured reliability and cost.

It would not establish:

- comparative superiority;
- domain transfer;
- autonomous governance-object discovery;
- one correct claim ontology;
- a universal governance loss;
- a general theory of intelligence.

---

## Pilot invariant

\[
\boxed{
\text{A representation is not yet a public instrument until independent users can instantiate it, revise it, and obtain defensible consequences without inventor arbitration.}
}
