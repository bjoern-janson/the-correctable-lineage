# Authority Allocation Under Incomplete Causal Understanding

## Status

This document compresses the Sidis, Ramanujan, Interface Theory, authority-acquisition, and prospective-representation threads into one corpus-level invariant.

It is an interpretive synthesis and methodological proposal.

It is not a validated universal law of intelligence, an empirical measure of vision, or an authorization to compare or deploy adaptive architectures.

---

## 1. Core invariant

The deepest recurring rule is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Evidence may increase authority only along dimensions that the evidence can identify.}
}
\]

Let \(e\) be an evidential event and let \(W_j\) be the operational authority assigned to claim dimension \(j\).

Then legitimate authority transfer requires an identified evidential path:

\[
e
\rightsquigarrow
W_j.
\]

Where no such path has been established:

\[
\frac{\partial W_j}{\partial e}
\approx
0.
\]

This is not a demand that every mechanism be completely understood before any result can be used.

It is a prohibition on unsupported authority spillover.

---

## 2. The general failure pattern

Across domains, the same structure appears:

\[
\text{local success}
\rightarrow
\text{global interpretation}
\rightarrow
\text{inflated authority}.
\]

Examples include:

### Ramanujan-type spillover

\[
\text{validated result}
\rightarrow
\text{assumed generative understanding}.
\]

### Sidis-type spillover

\[
\text{organized trace}
\rightarrow
\text{assumed endogenous correction}.
\]

### Vision-type spillover

\[
\text{compelling future representation}
\rightarrow
\text{assumed possibility, probability, desirability, reachability, or inevitability}.
\]

### Institutional spillover

\[
\text{one successful intervention}
\rightarrow
\text{general policy authority}.
\]

### AI spillover

\[
\text{useful output}
\rightarrow
\text{assumed understanding, reliability, autonomy, or alignment}.
\]

The common correction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Preserve access to the successful object while bounding the interpretation attached to it.}
}
\]

---

## 3. The hidden symmetry between discovery and vision

Ramanujan and prospective vision appear to concern different objects.

Ramanujan:

\[
\text{candidate mathematical structure}
\]

appears before:

\[
\text{accessible generative explanation}.
\]

Vision:

\[
\text{candidate future structure}
\]

appears before:

\[
\text{identified transition pathway and selection environment}.
\]

The shared topology is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{candidate resolution}
>
\text{current explanatory resolution}.
}
\]

The system can represent an object more sharply than it can yet justify its origin, scope, pathway, or reliability.

The appropriate response is neither rejection nor unrestricted adoption.

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{preserve access without granting unearned authority}.
}
\]

---

## 4. Typed knowledge state

For a candidate object \(y\), retain:

\[
\boxed{
K_y=(V,P,M,X,F)
}
\]

where:

- \(V\): result validity;
- \(P\): generative provenance;
- \(M\): generative or causal mechanism;
- \(X\): explanation fidelity;
- \(F\): future reliability and transfer envelope.

These dimensions do not automatically transfer authority to one another:

\[
V\neq P\neq M\neq X\neq F.
\]

A verified result may have:

\[
V=1,
\qquad
P=?,
\qquad
M=?,
\qquad
X=?,
\qquad
F=?
\]

without contradiction.

### Two-ledger presentation

For operational clarity, the five dimensions may be displayed in two linked ledgers:

#### Epistemic-content ledger

\[
E_y=(V,M,X).
\]

This asks:

- Is the result true?
- What process makes or made it true?
- Does the explanation correspond to that process?

#### Transfer-and-deployment ledger

\[
D_y=(P,F).
\]

This asks:

- From which resources, searches, filters, and historical path did it arise?
- Under which conditions should it continue to be trusted or used?

This grouping is presentational rather than ontological.

Provenance remains epistemically relevant, and mechanism remains operationally relevant.

The five underlying dimensions must remain independently updateable.

---

## 5. Typed authority state

The corresponding authority vector is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf W_y
=
(W_V,W_P,W_M,W_X,W_F).
}
\]

Each evidential event produces a scoped update:

\[
\Delta\mathbf W_y
=
J_E\Delta\mathbf E,
\]

where \(J_E\) is an evidence-to-authority addressability map.

The desired topology is sparse:

- proof should strongly update \(W_V\);
- generation logs should update \(W_P\);
- interventions on the generator should update \(W_M\);
- temporal and counterfactual trace tests should update \(W_X\);
- transfer and calibration tests should update \(W_F\).

The no-spillover rule is:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta W_i
\not\Rightarrow
\Delta W_j
\quad
\text{without an independently identified evidential edge }i\rightarrow j.
}
\]

Two especially dangerous laundering paths are:

\[
W_V\rightarrow W_M
\]

and:

\[
W_V\rightarrow W_F.
\]

A correct result does not prove the discoverer's method.

A successful prediction does not establish broad future reliability.

## 6. The true unit of inheritance

A correctable lineage should not inherit a bare claim.

It should inherit an evidence-bearing object:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_y
=
\left\langle
 y,
 \mathcal E_y,
 P_y,
 M_y,
 X_y,
 \mathcal D_y,
 \mathcal L_y,
 \mathbf W_y
\rightangle
}
\]

where:

- \(y\): claim, model, representation, or proposed future;
- \(\mathcal E_y\): validation history;
- \(P_y\): provenance state;
- \(M_y\): mechanism status;
- \(X_y\): explanation-fidelity status;
- \(\mathcal D_y\): demonstrated reliability domain;
- \(\mathcal L_y\): known limits and unresolved contradictions;
- \(\mathbf W_y\): current typed authority.

This supports asymmetric inheritance:

\[
\boxed{
\text{inherit validated information faster than unjustified explanation.}
}
\]

More explicitly:

- preserve the verified result;
- preserve uncertainty about its generator;
- preserve provenance gaps;
- preserve the scope of validation;
- preserve failed transfer tests;
- continue testing mechanism and reliability;
- do not allow later narratives to overwrite earlier evidential status.

## 7. Prospective representation operator

Creativity and vision can be represented as a prospective representation operator:

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t:
(M_t,\mathcal A_t,\mathcal C_t)
\rightarrow
\{\rho_{t,1}^{F},\rho_{t,2}^{F},\ldots\}
}
\]

where:

- \(M_t\): current world model;
- \(\mathcal A_t\): available action space;
- \(\mathcal C_t\): known constraints;
- \(\rho_{t,i}^{F}\): candidate representation of a future state and pathway.

The operator has causal leverage when a representation changes reachable action:

\[
\rho_{t,i}^{F}
\rightsquigarrow
\Delta\mathcal A_{\mathrm{reachable}}.
\]

The connection to interface-induced reachability is:

\[
\text{unrepresented possibility}
\approx
\text{operationally unavailable action}
\]

for a bounded system.

But:

\[
\boxed{
\text{representation expansion}
\neq
\text{reality expansion}.
}
\]

A representation opens a search direction.

Reality determines whether the represented state and pathway exist under the declared constraints.

---

## 8. Future-resolution vector

A future representation should not receive one undifferentiated vision score.

Use:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf R_f
=
(R_{\mathrm{poss}},
R_{\mathrm{prob}},
R_{\mathrm{value}},
R_{\mathrm{path}},
R_{\mathrm{coord}},
R_{\mathrm{select}},
R_{\mathrm{rev}})
}
\]

where:

### Possibility resolution \(R_{\mathrm{poss}}\)

Can the represented state exist under the declared constraints?

### Probability resolution \(R_{\mathrm{prob}}\)

How likely is it under declared policies, environments, and uncertainty?

### Value resolution \(R_{\mathrm{value}}\)

For whom, relative to which values, and under which distribution of costs and benefits is it desirable?

### Path resolution \(R_{\mathrm{path}}\)

Which causal transitions could produce it?

### Coordination resolution \(R_{\mathrm{coord}}\)

Can relevant agents interpret and act through the representation?

### Selection resolution \(R_{\mathrm{select}}\)

Which feedback and competition mechanisms determine whether this trajectory survives relative to alternatives?

### Revision resolution \(R_{\mathrm{rev}}\)

Which observations, failures, or rival outcomes would reduce its authority?

The new selection coordinate matters because a reachable future may still lose against alternatives.

A proposed technology, institution, strategy, organism, or cultural representation exists within a competitive environment.

Thus a prospective representation should ask not only:

> Can this happen?

but also:

> Which selection process would make this trajectory persist rather than its competitors?

## 9. Vision authority vector

A candidate future \(\rho^F\) should receive separate authority over each dimension:

\[
\mathbf W_{\rho}
=
(W_{\mathrm{poss}},
W_{\mathrm{prob}},
W_{\mathrm{value}},
W_{\mathrm{path}},
W_{\mathrm{coord}},
W_{\mathrm{select}},
W_{\mathrm{rev}}).
\]

A vivid representation may have high coordination power while having weak authority over possibility or probability.

A technically possible future may have no demonstrated selection advantage.

A probable future may be normatively unacceptable.

A desirable future may lack a reachable path.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{compelling}
\neq
\text{possible}
\neq
\text{probable}
\neq
\text{desirable}
\neq
\text{reachable}
\neq
\text{selectable}
\neq
\text{inevitable}.
}
\]

## 10. Expanded lineage cycle

The corpus-level process becomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Generate}
\rightarrow
\text{Evaluate}
\rightarrow
\text{Allocate authority}
\rightarrow
\text{Select}
\rightarrow
\text{Act}
\rightarrow
\text{Correct}
\rightarrow
\text{Inherit}.
}
\]

A more explicit loop is:

\[
E_t
\rightarrow
O_t
\rightarrow
M_t
\rightarrow
\Gamma_t
\rightarrow
\rho_t^F
\rightarrow
A_t
\rightarrow
E_{t+1}^*
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathbf W_{t+1}
\rightarrow
M_{t+1}.
\]

This contains two coupled engines.

### Generative engine

\[
M_t
\rightarrow
\Gamma_t
\rightarrow
\rho_t^F.
\]

It expands candidate model and future space.

### Corrective-governance engine

\[
E_{t+1}^*
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathbf W_{t+1}
\rightarrow
M_{t+1}.
\]

It determines which dimensions of which representations retain the right to govern future action.

Generation without corrective governance becomes authority inflation.

Correction without generation becomes increasingly efficient adaptation within an inherited possibility space.

## 11. Selection as a distinct layer

The unified sequence is not merely:

\[
\text{generate}
\rightarrow
\text{inherit}.
\]

It requires:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Generate}
\rightarrow
\text{Validate}
\rightarrow
\text{Scope authority}
\rightarrow
\text{Select}
\rightarrow
\text{Inherit}.
}
\]

Selection resolution operates at two levels.

### Claim selection

Which models, explanations, and hypotheses retain epistemic authority?

### Trajectory selection

Which actions, technologies, institutions, organisms, and coordinated futures persist in the environment?

These can diverge.

A false but persuasive representation may win social selection.

A valid but inaccessible representation may lose institutional selection.

A correctable lineage must measure and govern both.

## 12. Authority acquisition rate

The corpus can now state intelligence in a more governance-centered form:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Intelligence includes controlling the rate and scope at which representations acquire the right to shape reality.}
}
\]

Let:

\[
\alpha_j(t)
=
\frac{dW_j}{dt}
\]

be the authority-acquisition rate for dimension \(j\).

The goal is not universally slow authority acquisition.

Excessively slow updates cause paralysis.

The requirement is calibrated acquisition:

\[
\alpha_j(t)
\propto
\text{strength, relevance, independence, scope, and reversibility of evidence for }j.
\]

A useful governance target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{authority growth should not outrun evidential identification.}
}
\]

This adds scope as well as rate.

A result may deserve immediate local authority and no domain-wide authority.

## 13. New failure modes

### Vision laundering

A vivid future receives possibility, probability, or inevitability authority without corresponding evidence.

### Inevitability laundering

A selected or popular trajectory is represented as the only possible trajectory.

### Selection blindness

A future model identifies a state and path but omits the competitive processes determining whether it persists.

### Selection naturalization

A trajectory's current competitive advantage is treated as proof that it is desirable or universally optimal.

### Authority velocity overshoot

A model's authority grows faster than relevant evidence can discriminate its claims.

### Scope overshoot

Local validation expands into unrelated domains, horizons, populations, or interventions.

### Coordination-truth conflation

A representation's ability to coordinate agents is treated as evidence of truth.

### Self-fulfilling validation

A representation causes behavior that partially realizes itself, then treats the resulting resemblance as independent predictive confirmation.

### Prospectivity capture

One future representation reorganizes language, incentives, and observation interfaces until alternatives cannot be expressed or tested.

### Inheritance flattening

A lineage inherits a conclusion while erasing its validation history, provenance uncertainty, mechanism status, reliability limits, and authority type.

## 14. Unified stress-test table

| Stress test | Valid local observation | Invalid authority leap |
|---|---|---|
| Sidis | organized or temporally structured behavior | therefore endogenous reverse adaptation |
| Ramanujan | independently validated mathematical result | therefore identified generation mechanism |
| Vision | compelling or coordination-enabling future representation | therefore possible, probable, desirable, selectable, or inevitable |
| AI output | useful answer or prediction | therefore understanding, autonomy, alignment, or broad reliability |
| Institution | anomaly logging or policy statement | therefore operational authority revision |

The shared correction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{grant local authority to the validated dimension and preserve uncertainty everywhere else.}
}
\]

## 15. Revised corpus-level question

The broad question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How can a lineage generate, evaluate, select, and inherit representations whose authority remains proportional to evidence supporting their exact scope?}
}
\]

This includes:

- present-world models;
- prospective futures;
- scientific hypotheses;
- mathematical discoveries;
- causal explanations;
- policies;
- adaptive mechanisms;
- inherited representations.

The object is therefore not only adaptation or vision.

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{authority allocation under incomplete causal understanding}.
}
\]

---

## 16. Claim boundary

This synthesis does not establish that:

- authority can be represented by one universal scalar or vector;
- every evidence-to-authority edge is identifiable;
- all future dimensions are separable in every domain;
- causal understanding is always required for safe deployment;
- validated opacity is harmless;
- vision is the highest-leverage human capacity;
- high causal impact is beneficial;
- selection success establishes truth or value;
- calibrated authority guarantees alignment or safety.

The framework supplies distinctions and governance constraints.

It does not supply universal weights, values, or deployment thresholds.

## Final compression

\[
\boxed{
\text{Evidence may increase authority only along dimensions it can identify.}
}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Preserve the discovery. Bound the interpretation. Test the mechanism. Scope the reliability.}
}
\]

For prospective representations:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Generate possibilities, identify their selection environment, and keep their authority subordinate to reality.}
}
\]

The deepest intelligence claim is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Intelligence is not only producing better models of reality; it is controlling the rate and scope at which those models acquire the right to shape reality.}
}
