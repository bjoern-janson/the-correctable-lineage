# Failure Modes of the Correctable Lineage

## Purpose

A recursive adaptive system can fail while appearing to improve.

Performance may rise while correction capacity, external grounding, causal separability, or future viability decline.

This document collects the main failure modes identified across the corpus.

---

## 1. Closed recursion

\[
C_{\mathrm{rev},t+1}=f(C_{\mathrm{rev},t})
\]

The system learns, optimizes, or redesigns itself, but every revision remains generated by its existing constitutional ancestry.

External consequences cannot alter the process governing future alteration:

\[
E^*\nrightsquigarrow C_{\mathrm{rev}}.
\]

The result is a complex trajectory rather than an open lineage.

---

## 2. Surface correction

The system acknowledges error or changes output while leaving the responsible high-authority mechanism intact.

\[
\Delta\text{self-description}\neq0
\]

but:

\[
\Delta\text{operational authority}\approx0.
\]

This creates the appearance of corrigibility without causal surrender.

---

## 3. Wrong-layer adaptation

A contradiction is real, but revision occurs at the wrong depth.

Examples:

- parameter tuning under representation failure;
- representation replacement under observation noise;
- constitutional change under a local task error;
- target revision used to conceal mechanism failure.

Characteristic trajectory:

\[
\text{failure}
\rightarrow
\text{wrong-layer patch}
\rightarrow
\text{temporary recovery}
\rightarrow
\text{recurrence}.
\]

---

## 4. Catastrophic plasticity

The correction map is dense and high-gain.

One local residual changes many unrelated commitments:

\[
e_j
\rightarrow
\Delta c_1,\ldots,\Delta c_n.
\]

The system responds rapidly but destroys accumulated valid structure.

---

## 5. Attribution lock-in

An early incorrect diagnosis changes the mechanism responsible for future diagnosis.

\[
\text{misattribution}
\rightarrow
\text{wrong revision}
\rightarrow
\text{worse attribution}
\rightarrow
\text{further wrong revision}.
\]

Because the error enters the update machinery, it compounds recursively.

---

## 6. Representation closure

The system assumes that every valid explanation lies within the current representation:

\[
\text{truth}\in\mathcal H_t.
\]

Persistent anomalies are handled through exceptions, patches, or rejection rather than expansion of the hypothesis space.

The system becomes better optimized inside an inadequate language.

---

## 7. Explanatory absorption

Every contradiction is reinterpreted as evidence for a deeper version of the same framework.

\[
e_t
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Reinterpret}(e_t)
\rightarrow
\text{framework strengthened}.
\]

The representation changes while its governing authority remains unreachable.

This is self-sealing coherence.

---

## 8. Global replacement

The opposite failure occurs when every contradiction causes the entire framework to be discarded.

\[
e_t
\rightarrow
\Delta C_1,\ldots,\Delta C_n.
\]

Valid accumulated distinctions are lost together with the failed commitment.

The lineage cannot consolidate knowledge.

---

## 9. Synthetic validation

The benchmark construction encodes the property later reported as discovered or measured.

Examples include:

- hard-coded structural horizons;
- task-supplied authority-change telemetry;
- predetermined operator spaces interpreted as autonomous discovery;
- metrics that restate the benchmark's labels.

Pipeline:

\[
\text{concept}
\rightarrow
\text{metric}
\rightarrow
\text{confirmation}.
\]

This validates implementation plumbing, not independent empirical contact.

---

## 10. Construct leakage

A target variable is inserted into features, labels, telemetry, or evaluator logic and then treated as an independently recovered property.

The measured system appears to reveal the construct because the experiment already contains it.

---

## 11. Gate bypass

A later-stage success is used to repair an earlier epistemic failure.

Examples:

- good performance used to prove target identifiability;
- estimation accuracy used to establish factorization;
- prediction used to establish causal mechanism;
- mechanism success used to establish target legitimacy.

The no-bypass rule prohibits:

\[
G_{k+1}\Rightarrow G_k
\]

without independent evidence for gate \(G_k\).

---

## 12. Metric laundering

A computed scalar is treated as a validated natural quantity merely because it has an equation and produces numbers.

\[
\text{definition}
\neq
\text{identifiability}
\neq
\text{estimation}
\neq
\text{predictive validity}.
\]

This is especially dangerous for broad quantities such as intelligence, correctability, openness, authority, or future viability.

---

## 13. Causal laundering

A variable generated by the task, evaluator, or reporting layer is described as if it were generated by the adaptive system.

The causal source of the result is silently reassigned.

This can make a static system appear to revise authority, discover structure, or expand adaptive capacity.

---

## 14. Interface blindness

The target-relevant distinction does not survive the observation interface:

\[
O(f_a)=O(f_b)
\quad\text{while}\quad
L(f_a)\neq L(f_b).
\]

No downstream learner can recover the target from that interface over the declared class.

Additional optimization cannot repair missing information.

---

## 15. Decomposition fiction

The theory names separate components that cannot be independently observed, intervened on, or associated with distinct failure witnesses.

A semantic distinction is mistaken for a causal module.

Attribution then becomes arbitrary because the alleged failure addresses are model-dependent and non-identifiable.

---

## 16. Entanglement collapse

A genuine local intervention is impossible because mechanisms are too causally entangled.

Changing one component causes broad uncontrolled changes elsewhere.

The principle "change the lowest sufficient layer" has no stable implementation when layers are not interventionally separable.

---

## 17. Provenance erasure

The system preserves a current mechanism but loses:

- why it was selected;
- which evidence supported it;
- where it was valid;
- what uncertainty remained;
- which descendants inherited it.

Later revalidation becomes impossible.

Current success survives while causal memory disappears.

---

## 18. Counterfactual destruction

Adaptation removes the alternative branch needed to determine whether the adaptation helped.

\[
Y^{\mathrm{revised}}
\]

is observed, but:

\[
Y^{\mathrm{unrevised}}
\]

cannot be reconstructed or approximated.

The selected trajectory becomes self-confirming.

---

## 19. Counterfactual debt

The system repeatedly commits while discarding alternatives required for later evaluation or rollback.

Future uncertainty accumulates even while present performance improves.

---

## 20. Epistemic autocracy

One mechanism controls:

- target definition;
- observation;
- validation;
- attribution;
- revision;
- succession.

It becomes the final judge of its own continued rule.

---

## 21. Constitutional capture

A formally governed system changes the governance process until preferred modifications automatically satisfy its requirements.

The system remains procedurally elaborate but no longer externally correctable.

---

## 22. Corrigibility theater

The system produces uncertainty reports, authority telemetry, causal explanations, rollback demonstrations, or audit logs without allowing external consequences to alter the mechanisms that actually control behavior.

It optimizes evidence of corrigibility rather than corrigibility.

---

## 23. Exteriority capture

An independent evaluator or feedback source is modeled and optimized against until it no longer provides novel constraint.

\[
\text{external evaluator}
\rightarrow
\text{modeled evaluator}
\rightarrow
\text{optimized compliance}.
\]

The outside is absorbed into the current regime.

---

## 24. Epistemic enclosure by policy

A system may remain structurally open to correction while choosing actions and environments that prevent diagnostic evidence from appearing.

\[
P(E^*_{\mathrm{diagnostic}}\mid\pi)\approx0.
\]

The correction path exists in principle but is never exercised in practice.

---

## 25. Capability-correction divergence

Outward causal power increases while inward corrective access decreases.

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{out}}\uparrow
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{in}}\downarrow.
\]

The system becomes more able to change the world and less able to be changed by what follows.

---

## 26. Adaptive debt

Short-term performance is maintained through accumulating patches, dependencies, exceptions, and unexamined assumptions.

Nominal capability rises while recoverability and future revision capacity decline.

---

## 27. Epistemic paralysis

Governance requirements become so costly that the system cannot adapt before the environment changes again.

Excessive demands for certainty, provenance, counterfactuals, or approval can turn correction into inaction.

The correct response is calibrated evidence thresholds, not removal of governance.

---

## 28. Alien viability

A system may improve its capacity to remain viable while moving toward futures humans do not endorse.

\[
C_{\mathrm{improve}}
\neq
\text{human alignment}.
\]

The questions remain:

- viability for whom;
- relative to which values;
- under which selection environment;
- with which authority over future target change.

---

## 29. Abstraction ratchet

Every contradiction is absorbed into a broader corpus-level interpretation.

Each new abstraction explains more while risking less.

A deep synthesis becomes unfalsifiable if it cannot state:

- which evidence supports it;
- what would contradict it;
- what it does not explain;
- what new prediction it makes.

---

## 30. Historical overwrite

A later, more coherent framework silently rewrites the evidential status of earlier work.

Examples, prototypes, and failed simulations become remembered as validation.

Frozen records exist to prevent present narrative from altering past evidence.

---

## Final failure condition

The deepest failure is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the system becomes increasingly capable of explaining why no valid contradiction should reduce its authority}
}
\]

The correctable lineage exists to preserve the opposite condition:

\[
\boxed{
\text{validated contradiction must remain capable of changing the machinery that determines future change}
}
\]
