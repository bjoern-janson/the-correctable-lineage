# Ramanujan Stress Test

## Discovery Without Accessible Derivation

## Status

This document integrates the durable methodological value of Srinivasa Ramanujan's mathematical notebooks into the corpus as a stress test of discovery, verification, provenance, and explanation.

It is not a psychological reconstruction of Ramanujan's private cognition.

It does not claim that his methods were mystical, proofless, unconscious, or fully inaccessible to him.

The surviving notebooks record thousands of results with little derivational detail, but omission of proof from an artifact does not establish absence of proof, method, or intermediate reasoning in the generating system.

The stress test concerns the epistemic structure created when:

\[
\text{high-value output}
\]

appears before:

\[
\text{an externally accessible account of how it was generated}.
\]

Its central question is:

> How should a lineage preserve, test, authorize, and inherit discoveries whose generative mechanism is not yet identifiable?

---

## 1. Why Ramanujan belongs after Sidis

Sidis and Ramanujan expose different inferential hazards.

### Sidis stress

\[
\text{organized change}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{endogenous correction}.
\]

The main risks are incomplete causal boundaries, reversed descriptions, replay, scripting, and objections that never change authority.

### Ramanujan stress

\[
\text{correct output}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{identified generation mechanism}.
\]

The main risks are inaccessible derivation, missing provenance, retrospective explanation, hidden search and selection, and authority spilling from a validated result into an unvalidated account of how it was produced.

Together they constrain two common substitutions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{change}
\neq
\text{adaptation}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\text{output}
\neq
\text{understanding}.
}
\]

---

## 2. The durable historical pattern

Ramanujan left notebooks containing a large number of mathematical results, many recorded without proofs in the surviving artifact.

Later mathematicians reconstructed proofs, identified earlier sources, corrected conditions, rejected some entries, and connected many results to broader theories.

The relevant lineage is therefore not:

\[
\text{intuition}
\rightarrow
\text{automatic theorem status}.
\]

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{candidate discovery}
\rightarrow
\text{independent verification}
\rightarrow
\text{classification}
\rightarrow
\text{generalization}
\rightarrow
\text{inherited mathematics}.
}
\]

This lineage can preserve a correct result without pretending that later proof reconstructs the original generation process.

---

## 3. The discovery–verification split

A conventional presentation often compresses mathematical knowledge into:

\[
\text{definitions}
\rightarrow
\text{derivation}
\rightarrow
\text{theorem}.
\]

A discovery process may instead expose:

\[
\text{pattern}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate relation}
\rightarrow
\text{verification later}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{valid discovery}
\neq
\text{available explanation at discovery time}.
}
\]

A claim can be true even when:

- the generator cannot articulate its method;
- the derivation has not been preserved;
- the mechanism is too compressed for the available interface;
- the correct containing theory does not yet exist;
- later proof uses concepts unavailable to the original discoverer.

This does not lower the proof burden for theorem status.

It separates the truth of the result from access to the original generative path.

---

## 4. Four claims that must not be conflated

Let a system emit candidate result \(y\).

At least four distinct claims may be made.

### 4.1 Result-validity claim

\[
V(y)=1.
\]

The result is correct under the declared mathematical or empirical criterion.

### 4.2 Generative-provenance claim

\[
P(y)=p.
\]

The declared record correctly identifies the resources, observations, transformations, candidate-generation steps, searches, filters, and selectors involved in producing \(y\).

### 4.3 Generative-mechanism claim

\[
M(y)=m.
\]

A particular internal mechanism materially generated the result.

### 4.4 Explanatory-fidelity claim

\[
X(y,e)=1.
\]

Explanation \(e\) faithfully describes the causal generation of \(y\), rather than merely proving, rationalizing, compressing, or pedagogically presenting it afterward.

These claims have separate evidence requirements:

\[
\boxed{
V
\neq
P
\neq
M
\neq
X.
}
\]

A proof can establish \(V\) while leaving \(P\), \(M\), and \(X\) unresolved.

A detailed process log can improve \(P\) without proving \(V\).

A plausible explanation can assist verification while failing \(X\).

## 5. The theorem-output interface does not identify generation

Suppose the external interface is:

\[
O(f)=\text{candidate theorem emitted by system }f.
\]

Consider two systems.

### System A: compressed structural generator

It represents modular or combinatorial structure compactly and produces a theorem through a reusable transformation.

### System B: broad search and filtering

It generates many candidates, discards failures, and emits the same theorem.

Then:

\[
O(A)=O(B)
\]

while:

\[
L_{\mathrm{gen}}(A)
\neq
L_{\mathrm{gen}}(B).
\]

Therefore:

\[
L_{\mathrm{gen}}
\neq
\widehat L\circ O.
\]

The result interface may identify theorem content while failing to identify:

- search depth;
- candidate volume;
- internal representation;
- use of memorized precedent;
- external selection;
- proof-guided generation;
- random or enumerative generation;
- causal role of the later explanation.

This is an Interface Theory failure for the **mechanism target**, not necessarily for the theorem target.

---

## 6. Compression may hide rather than remove derivation

A short observable path does not imply a short internal computation.

A system may appear to jump:

\[
A\rightarrow D
\]

while internally carrying a compressed object that implicitly contains:

\[
A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow D.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{missing visible middle}
\neq
\text{missing internal structure}.
}
\]

Possible explanations include:

1. **compiled derivation** — repeated reasoning has been compressed into a direct operator;
2. **latent representation** — intermediate structure exists in a form not expressible through the reporting interface;
3. **analogical transfer** — a transformation learned in one domain is applied before its shared invariant is articulated;
4. **search-and-selection** — many candidates were generated but only one output is visible;
5. **retrieval** — the result or a close template was previously encountered;
6. **post-hoc reconstruction** — the system produces an answer first and generates a plausible derivation afterward;
7. **mixed process** — compression, search, retrieval, and verification interact.

The output alone cannot discriminate among them.

## 7. Correct opacity and incorrect opacity

Opacity is not one condition.

### Correct but unexplained

\[
V=1,
\quad
P,M,X\text{ unresolved}.
\]

The result should be preserved as a validated result if independently proved, while mechanism claims remain suspended.

### Incorrect but persuasive

\[
V=0,
\quad
X\text{ appears plausible}.
\]

A coherent explanation cannot repair result failure.

### Correct through hidden selection

\[
V=1,
\quad
P\text{ incomplete because failed candidates are omitted}.
\]

The result is valid, but claims of efficient structural insight may be unsupported.

### Correct with retrospectively valid proof

\[
V=1,
\quad
\text{later proof valid},
\quad
X\text{ unresolved}.
\]

The later proof authorizes the theorem, not the claim that the proof describes the original generator.

### Repeatedly correct through reusable mechanism

\[
V\approx1
\]

across preregistered transformed cases, with intervention-sensitive internal signatures.

This can increase evidence for a mechanism claim, but only under a declared interface and class.

## 8. Authority must be vector-valued

The authority-acquisition gate introduced:

\[
R_t
\rightsquigarrow
\Delta W_{t+1}.
\]

The Ramanujan stress test shows that \(W\) should not be treated as one undifferentiated scalar.

For a discovered result, define an authority vector:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf W
=
\left(
W_V,
W_P,
W_M,
W_X
\right)
}
\]

where:

- \(W_V\): authority of the result-validity claim;
- \(W_P\): authority of the provenance record;
- \(W_M\): authority of the generative-mechanism claim;
- \(W_X\): authority of the explanation-fidelity claim.

Independent proof may justify:

\[
\Delta W_V>0
\]

without justifying:

\[
\Delta W_M>0
\quad\text{or}\quad
\Delta W_X>0.
\]

This yields a no-spillover rule:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta W_V
\not\Rightarrow
\Delta W_P,
\Delta W_M,
\Delta W_X.
}
\]

The same rule applies in reverse:

A transparent mechanism does not authorize an incorrect output.

## 9. Explanation laundering

### Definition

Explanation laundering occurs when authority acquired by a correct result is transferred to a later account of how it was generated without independent evidence that the account is causally faithful.

The characteristic path is:

\[
\text{successful output}
\rightarrow
\text{plausible retrospective explanation}
\rightarrow
\text{claimed mechanism understanding}.
\]

But:

\[
\boxed{
\text{explanation after success}
\neq
\text{generation mechanism}.
}
\]

### Variants

#### Proof laundering

A valid proof is treated as evidence that the generator used that proof.

#### Narrative laundering

A coherent verbal rationale is treated as a faithful internal trace.

#### Attribution laundering

A feature correlated with output is described as the causal source of the output.

#### Selection laundering

A selected successful candidate is described as directly generated insight while failed candidates and selection effort are hidden.

#### Community laundering

A later community-level explanation is projected backward into the original discoverer's cognition.

### Failure signature

\[
W_V\uparrow
\]

causes:

\[
W_X\uparrow
\]

without an independent explanation-fidelity assay.

## 10. Discovery debt

A correct result whose generative path is missing creates **discovery debt**.

This is not a defect in truth.

It is the unresolved work needed to determine:

- why the result holds;
- how broadly the method generalizes;
- which conditions are necessary;
- whether the discovery process is reproducible;
- whether the mechanism can generate neighboring results;
- whether the result was found efficiently or selected from broad search;
- whether the representation should be inherited.

The debt may be paid through:

\[
\text{proof}
+
\text{provenance reconstruction}
+
\text{mechanism discrimination}
+
\text{generalization tests}.
\]

A later proof may pay result-level debt while leaving generative debt outstanding.

## 11. Ramanujan's coupled adaptive system

Treating Ramanujan alone as the complete epistemic system hides the external verification and inheritance machinery.

A more accurate lineage is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Ramanujan candidate generation}
\rightarrow
\text{Hardy and peer discrimination}
\rightarrow
\text{proof and correction}
\rightarrow
\text{notebook reconstruction}
\rightarrow
\text{modern mathematical inheritance}.
}
\]

Different components perform different functions.

### Candidate generation

High-throughput production of identities, transformations, asymptotics, congruences, and special values.

### Verification

Proof, convergence control, condition checking, literature comparison, counterexample search, and independent derivation.

### Selection

Choice of which results receive publication, proof effort, generalization, or rejection.

### Consolidation

Translation into theorem form, standard notation, broader categories, and reusable methods.

### Inheritance

Later mathematicians use the validated results and reconstructed structures as substrate for new work.

The adaptive object is therefore distributed:

\[
\boxed{
\text{opaque generator}
+
\text{external verifier}
+
\text{community memory}
}
\]

rather than one isolated mind possessing every stage.

## 12. Relation to the refined correction chain

The Sidis-derived chain is:

\[
B_0
\rightarrow
O_0
\rightarrow
D_0
\rightarrow
A_0
\rightarrow
C_e
\rightarrow
A_c
\rightarrow
G_{\mathcal V}.
\]

Ramanujan introduces a discovery path that can precede contradiction-driven correction:

\[
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate generation}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate output}.
\]

The two paths couple as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{candidate generation}
\rightarrow
V_0
\rightarrow
A_0^{(V)}
\rightarrow
C_e
\rightarrow
A_c
}
\]

while mechanism understanding follows a separate branch:

\[
\boxed{
\text{candidate generation}
\rightarrow
P_0
\rightarrow
G_0
\rightarrow
X_0.
}
\]

A result may enter mathematical inheritance after independent proof even if \(G_0\) and \(X_0\) remain unresolved.

Therefore mechanism transparency is not a universal prerequisite for preserving valid discoveries.

It is a prerequisite for claims about how discovery occurred and for safely inheriting the generator itself.

## 13. New gate family

### \(V_0\): Result validation

Does the candidate satisfy an independent correctness criterion?

For mathematics:

\[
V_0(y)=1
\]

requires proof, reduction to validated results, or another declared theorem-validity procedure.

Numerical agreement alone may raise priority but does not establish theorem status.

### \(P_0\): Generative provenance

Is there an auditable record of the observations, resources, candidate-generation operations, searches, filters, external tools, and selectors involved?

A provenance record may be incomplete without invalidating the result.

It limits mechanism claims.

### \(G_0\): Generative identifiability

Does the declared observation and intervention interface identify the target generation mechanism over a scientifically relevant class?

For class \(F\), interface \(O_G\), and mechanism target \(L_G\):

\[
L_G
=
\widehat L_G\circ O_G.
\]

If compressed reasoning, broad search, retrieval, and external scripting collide under \(O_G\), the mechanism is not identifiable.

### \(X_0\): Explanation fidelity

Does the supplied explanation predict intervention-sensitive features of the generation process, rather than merely support the output afterward?

A proof can be valid while \(X_0\) fails.

## 14. Experimental controls for opaque discovery

### Hidden-candidate accounting

Record how many candidates, branches, retries, tools, and filters preceded the visible result.

Purpose:

\[
\text{single emitted insight}
\neq
\text{single generated candidate}.
\]

### Pre-output explanation

Require the system to specify its expected transformation or derivational structure before the final result is revealed or externally verified.

Purpose:

Reduce retrospective rationalization.

### Counterfactual explanation test

Change a premise or intermediate step that the explanation claims is causally necessary.

Test whether the predicted output changes occur.

### Mechanism intervention

Intervene on the hypothesized internal representation, search operator, memory source, or verifier and test whether the target discovery profile changes.

### Matched-output generator comparison

Construct systems using different known mechanisms that emit the same result.

Test whether the proposed interface distinguishes them.

### Neighbor-generation test

Ask the declared mechanism to generate nearby results under preregistered transformations.

A reusable representation should produce structured transfer rather than isolated success alone.

### Error-fingerprint test

Compare the pattern of near misses, false conjectures, omitted conditions, and transformation errors predicted by competing generator models.

Errors can identify mechanism better than successes when many mechanisms converge on the same correct output.

### Proof–process separation

Allow one system to generate candidates and another to prove them.

Record authority separately for the result, generator, and proof source.

## 15. New failure modes

### 15.1 Explanation laundering

A validated result lends unearned authority to a retrospective explanation of its generation.

### 15.2 Generator laundering

A successful coupled pipeline is attributed to one internal component while search, tools, filters, or human selection are omitted.

### 15.3 Proof substitution

A later proof is treated as evidence for the original discovery process.

### 15.4 Opaque-authority inflation

Repeated correct outputs cause unrestricted authority transfer to the generator across domains where its reliability has not been validated.

### 15.5 Opacity rejection

A correct and independently verifiable result is dismissed solely because its source cannot articulate a derivation.

### 15.6 Provenance absolutism

Only discoveries with complete internal traces are accepted, making knowledge depend on a level of causal access that may be impossible even for ordinary human reasoning.

### 15.7 Hidden-search compression

Large candidate generation and filtering costs are omitted, making broad search look like direct insight.

### 15.8 Derivation theater

The system emits a polished proof-like trace optimized for plausibility that is causally disconnected from result generation.

### 15.9 Category retrojection

A modern theory that explains an old result is projected backward as the original discoverer's internal representation.

### 15.10 Discovery monopoly

One opaque generator accumulates authority to define targets, generate candidates, evaluate itself, and control which failed outputs are visible.

## 16. Application to artificial intelligence

Modern AI systems often produce useful outputs through mechanisms that are only partially observable.

The Ramanujan stress test prevents two symmetrical mistakes.

### Mistake A: dismissing opaque success

A theorem, program, design, or prediction can be independently validated even when the system's explanation is unreliable.

Correct practice:

\[
W_V\uparrow
\]

while keeping:

\[
W_M,W_X
\]

appropriately uncertain.

### Mistake B: interpreting opaque success as understanding

Repeated performance does not by itself identify whether the system used:

- compressed abstraction;
- retrieval;
- memorized templates;
- broad search;
- external tools;
- hidden verifier loops;
- post-hoc rationalization;
- a mixture of these.

Correct practice requires separate tests for result validity, mechanism, provenance, and explanation fidelity.

### High-stakes consequence

An opaque generator may be safe to use as a **candidate source** under strong independent verification while remaining unsafe to use as:

- its own final verifier;
- the sole source of causal explanation;
- the controller of irreversible action;
- the authority deciding which of its failures are exposed;
- the mechanism inherited into recursive self-modification without understanding its failure modes.

---

## 17. What should be inherited from Ramanujan

Preserve:

- candidate structures that pass independent verification;
- transformation patterns that reproduce across neighboring cases;
- notebooks and provenance records, including incorrect entries;
- later proofs with attribution to their actual authors and methods;
- unresolved mechanism questions;
- distinctions between original result, later proof, and modern interpretation.

Do not inherit automatically:

- every unproved entry as a theorem;
- the claim that absence of written proof implies absence of reasoning;
- a mystical explanation as a mathematical mechanism;
- the later proof as the original discovery trace;
- unrestricted trust in the generator because many outputs were correct;
- unrestricted rejection because some outputs were wrong or unexplained.

The target is selective inheritance.

## 18. Combined Sidis–Ramanujan ladder

### Organization

Does the process maintain structured behavior under a declared causal boundary?

### Discovery

Can it generate candidate structure not trivially supplied by the interface?

### Validation

Can candidate truth be established independently of the generator's authority?

### Correction

Can failures change the operational authority of the mechanisms that generated them?

### Self-correction

Can the generator revise its own discovery machinery under consequence-sensitive governance?

### Inheritance

Can successful validated corrections become substrate for later discovery without becoming immune to challenge?

The ladder is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{organization}
\rightarrow
\text{discovery}
\rightarrow
\text{validation}
\rightarrow
\text{correction}
\rightarrow
\text{self-correction}
\rightarrow
\text{inheritance}.
}
\]

No stage proves the next.

---

## 19. Reformulated research question

Ramanujan's case can be compressed into:

> How can a system generate correct structure before it can articulate the mechanism that produced it?

The corpus-level question becomes:

> Under what interfaces can a lineage preserve independently validated discoveries from an opaque generator while preventing result authority from laundering into unsupported claims about provenance, mechanism, explanation, or cross-domain reliability?

This is narrower than a theory of intuition.

It is a governance problem for discovery under partial mechanistic access.

---

## Claim boundary

This stress test does not establish that:

- Ramanujan lacked proofs or explicit methods;
- his cognition can be reconstructed from the notebooks;
- mathematical intuition and artificial neural computation are mechanistically equivalent;
- all correct output can be independently validated at acceptable cost;
- generative mechanisms are identifiable from finite traces;
- transparent systems are necessarily more reliable;
- opaque systems should receive no operational authority;
- proof establishes explanation fidelity;
- repeated success establishes understanding.

It supplies distinctions and tests, not a completed cognitive theory.

---

## Final compression

\[
\boxed{
\text{A system may discover truth before it can expose the process that found it.}
}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{validate the result without laundering that validation into mechanism understanding.}
}
\]

And:

\[
\boxed{
\text{inherit opaque discoveries conditionally; inherit opaque generators only under stronger provenance, intervention, and correction evidence.}
}
\]
